Captions & Text Effects

Most viewers watch with the sound off before deciding whether to turn it on. Clear, well-timed captions and text aren't just accessibility features — they're often what keeps a viewer watching past the first few seconds. This guide covers how to add readable captions, animate text, and use typography to direct attention.

1. Auto-captions as a starting point, not a final draft

CapCut's auto-captions (tap the "Captions" tool, then "Auto Captions") transcribe your speech automatically, but they need a pass to correct misheard words, fix punctuation, and split long lines into readable chunks. Never publish auto-captions untouched — even a few misread words can undermine an otherwise polished edit.

2. Keeping caption text readable

Aim for no more than 2 short lines on screen at once, and keep each caption visible long enough to read comfortably — roughly 1 second per 3-4 words is a reasonable baseline. If a sentence is long, split it across two caption blocks rather than shrinking the font to fit it all on one line.

3. Styling captions for contrast

Captions need to stay legible over any background. In CapCut's text editor, add a semi-transparent background box behind the text (the "Background" option in text styling) or apply a bold outline/stroke around the letters. Stick to one font and color scheme throughout the edit — constantly changing caption styles feels inconsistent and distracts from the content.

4. Animating text for emphasis

CapCut's text animation presets (tap your text layer → Animation) let you apply entrance, loop, and exit animations. Use entrance animations like "Pop" or "Fade" for standard captions, and reserve more dramatic animations (bounce, glitch, typewriter) for a single emphasized word or key phrase you want to stand out — using dramatic animation on every line reduces its impact.

5. Using text to guide the eye

Text placement isn't just about legibility — it can direct where a viewer looks next. Position key text near the part of the frame you want to emphasize, and consider using scale or color to create a visual hierarchy: the most important word or phrase can be slightly larger or in your accent color, while supporting text stays smaller and neutral.

6. Timing text to match speech and beats

Captions that appear a beat late or linger after the speaker has moved on feel disconnected from the footage. Zoom into the timeline (pinch to zoom on the CapCut timeline) to align caption start and end points precisely with the audio waveform, rather than eyeballing it at the default zoom level.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Overcrowded captions: too much text on screen at once forces viewers to choose between reading and watching.
  • Inconsistent styling: switching fonts or colors between captions breaks visual continuity.
  • Ignoring timing accuracy: captions that drift out of sync with speech feel unpolished even if the wording is correct.

Once your captions are clean and well-timed, the next step is refining the overall look of your footage through color grading — covered in the next tutorial.
